# Mission and programs

Status: working draft, October 6, 2026. Direction reflects Shane's agreed goals; details are proposed operating choices.

## Mission

WLKR Labs builds and maintains excellent free software, helps people gain practical access to technology, and teaches them to use and create it. Rooted in biblical faith, we put Scripture access and study through PatriBible at the center of our work.

Short public line: **Free software. Useful technology. Biblical purpose.**

## What we mean

- **Excellent:** useful, accessible, clear, dependable, documented, and enjoyable to use. Preserve the existing craft principle: software, systems, and design belong together.
- **Free:** aim to keep core public tools and education available without mandatory payment. Distinguish free access from permission to reuse source code; publish licensing accurately for each project.
- **Accessible technology:** provide understandable installation, modest hardware requirements where practical, usable interfaces, and plain-language guidance. Hardware distribution is a possible later program, requiring a need, resources, and an operating plan.
- **Education:** publish real lessons, examples, and explanations that people can use independently. Measure learning outputs, not merely code output.
- **Biblical purpose:** make the Christian foundation visible and maintain PatriBible as the flagship. Offer public resources with respect and clarity.
- **Sustainable work:** reasonable pay for actual development and teaching can support continuity. Most available resources should serve creation, maintenance, access, and education.

Omarchy is an inspiration for ambition, software quality, and freely available tools. WLKR Labs develops its own mission and identity; it does not copy another project's governance or presume affiliation.

## Nonprofit theory

Free software is a way to deliver the mission. Religious education and public technical instruction are the strongest initial purposes to document. A future exemption application should describe actual beneficiaries, activities, outputs, and how funds further those purposes.

A useful public tool does not automatically establish 501(c)(3) eligibility. Build the evidence through real public education and religious resources, then select a legal structure that fits those activities. [IRS organizational test](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/organizational-test-internal-revenue-code-section-501c3), [public-benefit requirements](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/inurement-private-benefit-charitable-organizations).

## Staying useful

Start with maintenance of existing public capabilities. Choose one small educational output alongside that maintenance. Give each supported project a named maintainer, clear support scope, and documented next action.

At each review, record what became easier for a person: a repaired workflow, clearer installation, a useful Scripture study, or an understandable lesson. Treat download counts and donations as supporting information, not the mission itself.

Use the [monthly review](../templates/monthly-review.md) and [project record](../templates/project-record.md). If maintenance capacity ends, publish an honest status and preserve usable documentation instead of promising indefinite support.
